## Scoring & weight justification

`risk_score = round(min(rawWeightSum / 8, 1) * 100)`, where `rawWeightSum` is the
sum of the severity weights of every triggered rule. A claim is treated as flagged when
`risk_score >= 25`.

Each weight reflects how strongly a trigger implies *deliberate* fraud (versus a
coincidence or a clerical error), scaled by financial impact:

| Rule | Weight | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| duplicate_claim | 5 | An identical member+provider+date+diagnosis claim has almost no innocent explanation — it is direct double-billing. |
| phantom_billing | 5 | >30 claims in one day from a provider that averages a handful is physically implausible and inflates payouts at scale. |
| upcoding | 4 | A charge >2 SD above the norm for the procedure is a strong money+intent signal, but genuinely complex cases can be expensive — so 4, not 5. |
| amount_clustering | 4 | Landing just under the 50,000 auto-approval line is hard to explain as chance, but each such claim's loss is capped by that line — so 4, not 5. |
| unbundling | 3 | Billing components instead of the bundle is a known padding tactic, but procedures are sometimes legitimately separate encounters. |
| weekend_anomaly | 3 | A weekday-only provider suddenly operating on a weekend is a behavioural break, but weekends do happen — it needs corroboration. |
| rapid_resubmission | 2 | Legitimate follow-up visits and staged treatment look identical; only meaningful when stacked with other flags. |
| diagnosis_procedure_mismatch | 2 | Often a coding or data-entry error rather than fraud, and the reference mapping is deliberately incomplete — weak on its own. |

Low-weight rules are not "unimportant" — they are weak in isolation but push a claim over
the line when several fire together, which is the point of a weighted composite.

`DENOM = 8` sets the "definitely fraud" ceiling at roughly two of the heaviest rules
firing together. `THRESHOLD = 25` was picked from the sweep above as the lowest
value that keeps FPR ≤ 20% while maximising recall — insurance economics favour catching
fraud over sparing investigator time.
